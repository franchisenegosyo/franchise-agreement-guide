What a franchise agreement usually asks of you

A franchise agreement is the document that decides what you can and cannot do with your business for years, yet many Filipino buyers skim it because the brand seems trustworthy and the store is already being planned. Knowing what the main clauses usually say makes it far easier to ask the right questions before signing, and FranchiseNegosyo, a free Philippine franchise directory, publishes plain guides on each part of a typical agreement. None of this requires legal training to understand, and a buyer who knows the basics will get far more value from a lawyer's review than one who sees the agreement for the first time at the signing table.

What the agreement binds a franchisee to overall

Beyond the fee, a franchise agreement usually commits you to follow the operations manual, buy from approved suppliers, meet brand standards and accept inspections, and these duties often matter more day to day than any single payment. The guide on what a franchise agreement binds you to explains these obligations in plain language and points out the clauses that most often surprise new owners. Reading the whole agreement slowly, ideally with a lawyer, is the single most useful step before you commit. It is wise to ask for the full agreement well before any payment is due, so that you have time to read it at home and list every clause you do not fully understand.

How territory clauses protect or limit a branch

Many buyers assume they will be the only branch in their area, but that protection only exists if the agreement says so clearly, with a defined area and clear rules about new branches nearby. The guide on franchise territory clauses explains the difference between an exclusive territory, a protected radius and no protection at all. Knowing which one you are getting helps you judge whether the location you are investing in will still be worth the same if the brand keeps growing. If the brand plans to open more branches in your city, ask how it decides on new locations and whether existing owners are consulted, since that answer says a lot about how the relationship will work over time.

How royalties and advertising funds are usually charged

Most franchises charge ongoing fees after opening, usually a royalty based on sales and sometimes a separate contribution to a shared advertising fund, and both come out of your revenue whether the month is good or bad. The guide on how franchise royalties work shows how these fees are calculated and how they affect your margin, while the guide on advertising fund contributions explains what that money is meant to pay for and what reports you can reasonably ask to see. Factoring both into your monthly forecast gives a far more honest picture of what the business will earn. Ask also whether the fees are based on gross sales or on profit, since a royalty on gross sales is paid even in months when the store loses money after rent and wages.

How long the agreement lasts and what renewal involves

Franchise agreements run for a fixed term, often several years, and renewal is not always automatic, since some brands charge a renewal fee or require a store renovation before signing again. The guide on franchise term lengths and renewal rights explains what to look for so that you know whether you can keep the business you built. Many agreements also include a non-compete clause that limits what you can do after leaving, and the guide on non-compete clauses explains how these usually work. These terms deserve attention early, because the end of an agreement can arrive sooner than expected once the store is busy and the years start passing quickly.

What happens if the relationship ends early

Most agreements list the reasons a franchisor can end the contract early and what the owner must do afterward, such as removing signage, returning manuals and stopping use of the brand, often without any refund of fees already paid. The guide on what happens if a franchisor terminates your contract walks through these clauses and the questions to ask before you sign. Comparing several brands in the franchise listings helps you see which ones are worth reading an agreement for, and you can request free guidance from an independent advisor who can point out what to check before you commit. Knowing these terms in advance does not mean expecting the worst, but it does mean you will recognize a fair agreement when you see one and walk away from one that is not.
